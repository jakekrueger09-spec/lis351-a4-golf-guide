Learn the Basics Practice Plan

Three Things to Learn First

Golf gets easier to understand when you focus on a few simple ideas before worrying about every rule or club.

1

Choose a Simple Set of Clubs

You do not need every club in the bag when you are starting. A driver or fairway wood, a few irons, a wedge, and a putter give you enough options to learn the game.

2

Build a Repeatable Swing

Start with balance, a comfortable grip, and a smooth tempo. Making solid contact matters more than swinging as hard as possible while you are learning.

3

Learn How a Round Works

Each hole begins from a teeing area and moves toward the green. Your goal is to finish the hole in as few strokes as possible while following the rules and keeping pace.

Featured Skill: A Simple Pre-Shot Routine

Pick a target, aim the clubface, set your feet, take one last look, and make a smooth swing. Keeping the routine simple helps you focus on the shot.

A 30-Minute Practice Plan

A short practice session works best when you divide your time between different parts of the game.

  1. 5 minutes of short wedge shots.
  2. 10 minutes of iron shots.
  3. 5 minutes with a driver or fairway wood.
  4. 10 minutes of putting.

Basic Course Etiquette

Be ready when it is your turn, stay quiet while someone else is hitting, repair ball marks, replace divots when appropriate, and rake bunkers after using them.

If a shot might reach another person, yell "Fore!" right away. Beginners should also keep pace with the group ahead.

Ready for Your First Round?

Start with a short course or a relaxed nine-hole round. Focus on safe shots and keep score only if it helps you enjoy the experience.
